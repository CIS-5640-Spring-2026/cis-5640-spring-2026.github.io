Let’s practice analyzing the relationship between game mechanics and the player experience they create. Using the [Mechanics, Dynamics, Aesthetics framework](https://users.cs.northwestern.edu/~hunicke/MDA.pdf), analyze a game of your choosing. Try to aim for a total length of 2 pages!


1. Choose a game to analyze. The game can be digital or analog or physical but should be reasonably complex.

2. Write a *short* description of the game. You should describe
- The high level player experience. If you were the game director, how would you describe the vision of this game?
- The objective of the game
- The narrative premise of the game
- The procedure of the game. In brief, what is the player doing?

3. Choose three mechanics from this game. For each mechanic, provide a breakdown of
- The mechanics: what is the game “rule”?
    - This should be what literally is the code or rulebook enforcing?
    - For example “The force of gravity applies to all characters” or “Performing a headshot gives the player a 10 point bonus”

- The dynamics: what player behavior does this elicit?
    - Dynamics are the player’s response within the game environment. NOT what the system does, but how the player behaves as a result of the rules of the game.
    - For example, because diamonds have a low rarity, players will be more likely to be selective in when they use them, as opposed to a different resource.
    - For example, in games with a kill streak bonus, players are more likely to try to continue being aggressive after getting a single kill.

- The aesthetics: what emotional response does this evoke?
    - This is what YOU feel as you're playing the game when the Mechanics and Dynamics happen. You should be using emotion words when describing aesthetics.
    - For example, in Minecraft, when you fall from a high place and don’t have a bucket of water to break your fall, you may feel frustrated that your character will die or get a lot of damage.You may feel anxious navigating high-up terrain.


4. Write a brief reflection on how the three mechanics you’ve chosen work together to support the vision of the game. Or don’t!
